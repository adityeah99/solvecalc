Probability is the math of chance. It helps you answer questions like "How likely is it to rain tomorrow?" or "What are my chances of rolling a six?"

You can never know for sure what one roll of a die will give you. But probability tells you how likely each result is, and that turns out to be very useful.

## The basic formula

When every outcome is equally likely, the probability of something happening is:

**Probability = number of ways it can happen ÷ total number of possible outcomes**

**Example 1:** What is the probability of rolling a 4 on a normal six-sided die?

There is 1 way to roll a 4, and there are 6 possible outcomes. So the probability is **1/6**.

**Example 2:** What is the probability of rolling an even number?

The even numbers are 2, 4 and 6, so there are 3 ways. The probability is 3/6, which simplifies to **1/2**.

**Example 3:** A bag holds 3 red marbles, 5 blue marbles and 2 green marbles. You pick one without looking. What is the probability it's red?

There are 3 + 5 + 2 = 10 marbles in total, and 3 of them are red. So the probability is **3/10**.

## The 0 to 1 scale

Every probability is a number from 0 to 1.

- **0** means impossible. Rolling a 7 on a normal die has a probability of 0.
- **1** means certain. Rolling a number less than 7 has a probability of 1.
- **0.5** means an even chance, like a coin landing on heads.

You can write a probability as a fraction, a decimal or a percentage. So 1/4, 0.25 and 25% all mean the same thing.

If you ever get a probability bigger than 1 or less than 0, something has gone wrong. Go back and check your working.

## Complements: the chance it doesn't happen

Something either happens or it doesn't. Those two chances always add up to 1. The chance that something does **not** happen is called the **complement**.

**Probability it doesn't happen = 1 − probability it happens**

**Example:** The weather forecast says there's a 30% chance of rain tomorrow. The chance it stays dry is 100% − 30% = **70%**.

**Example:** The chance of rolling a 6 is 1/6. So the chance of not rolling a 6 is 1 − 1/6 = **5/6**.

**Example:** In the marble bag above, the chance of picking green is 2/10. So the chance of not picking green is 1 − 2/10 = 8/10, which simplifies to **4/5**.

## Independent events: multiply

Two events are **independent** if one doesn't change the chance of the other. Flipping a coin and rolling a die are independent. The coin has no effect on the die.

To find the chance that two independent events **both** happen, multiply their probabilities.

**Example:** You flip a coin and roll a die. What is the chance of getting heads **and** a 6?

The chance of heads is 1/2. The chance of a 6 is 1/6. So the chance of both is 1/2 × 1/6 = **1/12**.

You can check this by listing every result. There are 2 coin results and 6 die results, which makes 2 × 6 = 12 pairs. Only one pair, heads with a 6, is the one you want. So the chance is 1 out of 12.

Rolling two dice and getting two sixes works the same way: 1/6 × 1/6 = **1/36**.

**Putting it together:** What's the chance of getting at least one head when you flip a coin 3 times?

Counting every way to get "at least one" is fiddly, so use the complement instead. The only way to get no heads at all is tails, tails, tails. That has a chance of 1/2 × 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/8. So the chance of at least one head is 1 − 1/8 = **7/8**.

One last thing: coins and dice have no memory. If a coin lands on tails five times in a row, the chance of heads on the next flip is still 1/2. Each flip is independent of the ones before.
