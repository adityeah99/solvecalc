Geometry is the math of shapes and space. You use it more often than you might think: when you work out how much paint covers a wall, whether a sofa will fit through a door, or how much water fills a fish tank.

This lesson covers four big ideas: perimeter and area, circles, the Pythagorean theorem, and volume.

## Perimeter and area

These two get mixed up a lot, so it helps to keep them clear.

- **Perimeter** is the distance all the way around the outside of a shape. Think of a fence around a garden.
- **Area** is the amount of flat space inside a shape. Think of the grass inside the fence.

For a rectangle that is 8 m long and 5 m wide:

- Perimeter = 8 + 5 + 8 + 5 = **26 m**. You can also write this as 2 × (8 + 5).
- Area = length × width = 8 × 5 = **40 m²**.

Notice the units. Perimeter is a length, so it's in meters (m). Area counts squares, so it's in square meters (m²). The little 2 is a clue that you multiplied two lengths together.

For a triangle, the area is half of base × height. A triangle with a base of 10 cm and a height of 6 cm has an area of ½ × 10 × 6 = **30 cm²**. It's half because a triangle has exactly half the area of a rectangle with the same base and height.

## Circles

A circle has a few special parts:

- The **radius** (r) goes from the center to the edge.
- The **diameter** (d) goes all the way across, through the center. It's twice the radius.
- The **circumference** is the distance around the circle. It's the circle's perimeter.

Circles use a special number called **pi**, written π. It's about 3.14. If you divide any circle's circumference by its diameter, you always get π.

The two formulas you need are:

- Circumference = 2 × π × r (or π × d)
- Area = π × r²

**Example:** A circle has a radius of 5 cm.

- Circumference = 2 × π × 5 = 10π ≈ **31.42 cm**
- Area = π × 5² = π × 25 ≈ **78.54 cm²**

Watch out for the most common mistake: using the diameter in the area formula. A pizza that is 30 cm across has a radius of 15 cm. Its area is π × 15² = π × 225 ≈ **706.86 cm²**. If you used 30 by mistake, your answer would be four times too big.

## The Pythagorean theorem

This rule only works for **right triangles**. These are triangles with one 90° angle, a square corner. The longest side, opposite the square corner, is called the **hypotenuse**.

If the two shorter sides are a and b, and the hypotenuse is c, then:

**a² + b² = c²**

**Example:** A right triangle has shorter sides of 3 cm and 4 cm. How long is the hypotenuse?

3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25. So c² = 25, and c = √25 = **5 cm**.

You can also use it backwards to find a shorter side. A 13 m ladder leans against a wall, with its foot 5 m away from the wall. How high up the wall does it reach?

The ladder is the hypotenuse. So the height squared is 13² − 5² = 169 − 25 = 144. The height is √144 = **12 m**.

One more handy fact about triangles: the three angles inside any triangle always add up to 180°.

## Volume

Area measures flat space. **Volume** measures how much space a 3D shape takes up, like how much water a box could hold. It's measured in cubic units, like cm³.

For a box shape (a cuboid), multiply length × width × height. A box that is 4 cm by 3 cm by 2 cm has a volume of 4 × 3 × 2 = **24 cm³**.

A cube has all its sides the same length. A cube with sides of 5 cm has a volume of 5 × 5 × 5 = **125 cm³**.

For a cylinder, like a can of soup, find the area of the circle on the end and multiply by the height: π × r² × h. A can with a radius of 3 cm and a height of 10 cm holds π × 9 × 10 = 90π ≈ **282.74 cm³**.

A useful fact for real life: 1,000 cm³ is exactly 1 liter. So that can holds a little over a quarter of a liter.
