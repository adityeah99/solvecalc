Fractions, decimals and percentages can look like three different topics. They're really three ways of writing the same thing: a part of a whole.

Half a pizza can be written as 1/2, as 0.5 or as 50%. Once you can move between these three, a lot of everyday math gets easier, from sharing food to working out discounts.

## What a fraction means

A fraction has two numbers. The bottom number, the **denominator**, tells you how many equal parts the whole is cut into. The top number, the **numerator**, tells you how many of those parts you have.

If a pizza is cut into 8 equal slices and you eat 3, you have eaten 3/8 of the pizza. The 8 slices make the whole, and 3 of them are yours. That leaves 5/8 for everyone else.

## Equivalent fractions and simplifying

Different fractions can show the same amount. 1/2, 2/4 and 4/8 are all exactly half. These are called **equivalent fractions**.

You make an equivalent fraction by multiplying or dividing the top and bottom by the same number. Doing the same thing to both keeps the value the same.

To **simplify** a fraction, divide the top and bottom by the biggest number that goes into both. This number is called the greatest common divisor, or GCD.

**Example:** Simplify 18/24.

The biggest number that divides both 18 and 24 is 6. So 18 ÷ 6 = 3 and 24 ÷ 6 = 4, which gives **3/4**.

If you don't spot the biggest number straight away, that's fine. You can divide in smaller steps instead: 18/24 → 9/12 → 3/4.

## Adding and subtracting fractions

When the bottoms are the same, add the tops and keep the bottom.

2/7 + 3/7 = **5/7**

When the bottoms are different, first rewrite the fractions so they share a bottom. This is called finding a **common denominator**.

**Example:** 1/3 + 1/4

A number that both 3 and 4 go into is 12. So 1/3 = 4/12 and 1/4 = 3/12. Now add: 4/12 + 3/12 = **7/12**.

A very common mistake is to add the tops and add the bottoms, which gives 2/7. That can't be right. Adding 1/4 to 1/3 should give more than 1/3, but 2/7 is actually less than 1/3.

Subtracting works the same way. For 5/6 − 1/4, use 12 as the common bottom: 10/12 − 3/12 = **7/12**.

Multiplying is easier still. Multiply the tops, then multiply the bottoms: 2/3 × 3/5 = 6/15, which simplifies to **2/5**.

## Turning fractions into decimals

A fraction is really a division. The line in the middle means "divided by". So to change a fraction into a decimal, divide the top by the bottom.

- 3/4 = 3 ÷ 4 = **0.75**
- 2/5 = 2 ÷ 5 = **0.4**
- 1/3 = 1 ÷ 3 = **0.333...**, where the 3s go on forever

That last one is called a **repeating decimal**. We usually round it, for example to 0.33.

## Percentages

"Percent" means "out of 100". So 35% just means 35 out of 100, which is 35/100 or 0.35.

To change a decimal into a percentage, multiply by 100. So 0.75 becomes **75%**. To go the other way, divide by 100.

Here are some you'll see all the time:

| Fraction | Decimal | Percentage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1/2 | 0.5 | 50% |
| 1/4 | 0.25 | 25% |
| 3/4 | 0.75 | 75% |
| 1/5 | 0.2 | 20% |
| 1/10 | 0.1 | 10% |

**Finding a percentage of an amount:** What is 20% of $60? Change 20% into a decimal, 0.2, then multiply: 0.2 × 60 = **$12**.

**Percentage change:** A game's price goes up from $40 to $50. What is the percentage increase? The increase is $50 − $40 = $10. Divide it by the **original** price: 10 ÷ 40 = 0.25, which is **25%**.

Be careful to divide by the starting value. If you divide by the new price by mistake, you get 10 ÷ 50 = 0.2, or 20%, which is wrong.
