The area of a shape is how much flat space it covers. For a circle, that could be how much cheese fits on a pizza or how much grass is inside a round flower bed.

There is one short formula you need, and once you know which number to put in, the rest is just multiplying.

## The formula

**A = πr²**

- A is the area.
- π (pi) is a number a little over 3. It is about 3.14159. Most calculators have a π button.
- r is the **radius**, the distance from the center of the circle to its edge.
- r² means r × r.

So in words: area = pi × radius × radius.

## Radius and diameter

The **diameter** is the distance all the way across the circle, through the center. It is twice the radius.

- diameter = 2 × radius
- radius = diameter ÷ 2

Many real objects are described by their diameter. A "12-inch pizza" is 12 inches across, so its radius is 6 inches. Always check which one you have before you start.

## Example: a 12-inch pizza

1. The pizza is 12 inches across, so the diameter is 12 in.
2. Find the radius: 12 ÷ 2 = 6 in
3. Square the radius: 6² = 6 × 6 = 36
4. Multiply by π: A = π × 36 ≈ 113.10
5. The pizza covers about 113.10 square inches.

Area is always measured in **square units**, like in², cm² or m². That is because you are measuring a flat surface, not a length.

## Example: a round garden pond

A pond is 10 meters across. How much surface area does the water have?

1. Diameter = 10 m, so radius = 10 ÷ 2 = 5 m
2. Square the radius: 5² = 25
3. Multiply by π: A = 25π ≈ 78.54
4. The pond has an area of about 78.54 m².

If you leave the answer as 25π m², that is the exact answer. The decimal is a rounded version.

## Example: one big pizza or two small ones?

Is one 12-inch pizza more food than two 8-inch pizzas?

1. From above, one 12-inch pizza is about 113.10 in².
2. An 8-inch pizza has radius 4 in. Its area is π × 4² = 16π ≈ 50.27 in².
3. Two of them: 2 × 50.27 ≈ 100.53 in².
4. So the single 12-inch pizza is bigger, by about 12.57 in².

This surprises people because 8 + 8 = 16, which is more than 12. But area depends on the radius **squared**, so a small increase in size makes a big increase in area.

## Why the formula works

Imagine cutting a circle into lots of thin slices, like a pizza, and laying them side by side, pointing up and down in turn. They form a shape that is almost a rectangle.

- Its height is the radius, r.
- Its width is half the distance around the circle. The full distance around is 2πr, so half is πr.

The area of a rectangle is width × height, so this is πr × r = πr². The thinner the slices, the closer the shape gets to a perfect rectangle.

## Working backwards: finding the radius

If you know the area, you can find the radius by undoing each step. Suppose a circle has an area of 50 cm².

1. Divide by π: 50 ÷ π ≈ 15.92
2. Take the square root: √15.92 ≈ 3.99
3. The radius is about 3.99 cm.

## Mistakes to watch for

- **Using the diameter instead of the radius.** For the 12-inch pizza, using 12 instead of 6 would give an answer four times too big.
- **Squaring π instead of r.** The formula is π × r × r, not π × π × r.
- **Mixing up area and circumference.** The distance around a circle is 2πr. The space inside it is πr².
- **Forgetting square units.** An area answer should say cm², m² or similar.

## Try it yourself

1. Find the area of a circle with radius 3 cm.
2. A round trampoline is 14 feet across. Find its area.

**Answers:** 1) π × 3² = 9π ≈ 28.27 cm². 2) The radius is 7 ft, so π × 7² = 49π ≈ 153.94 ft².
