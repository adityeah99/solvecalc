Picture this: you're at a pizza place and there's a deal on the board. One 12-inch pizza, or two 8-inch pizzas, same price. Which is more food?

Your gut says two pizzas must win. Your gut is wrong, and by the end of this article you'll prove it with thirty seconds of math.

All you need is one short formula. The multiplying is easy. The tricky part is knowing which number goes where, so we'll go slow there.

## The formula

**A = πr²**

- A is the area.
- π (pi) is a number a little over 3, about 3.14159. Most calculators have a π button.
- r is the **radius**, the distance from the center of the circle to its edge. r² means r × r.

In plain words: area = pi × radius × radius.

## Radius and diameter: get this right first

The **diameter** is the distance all the way across the circle, straight through the center. It's twice the radius.

- diameter = 2 × radius
- radius = diameter ÷ 2

Here's the thing I tell my students every year: half of all circle-area mistakes happen right here, before a single multiplication. Real objects are usually described by their diameter. A "12-inch pizza" is 12 inches across, so its radius is 6. Always check which one you're holding before you start.

## Example: a 12-inch pizza

1. The pizza is 12 inches across, so the diameter is 12 in.
2. Find the radius: 12 ÷ 2 = 6 in
3. Square the radius: 6² = 6 × 6 = 36
4. Multiply by π: A = π × 36 ≈ 113.10
5. The pizza covers about 113.10 square inches.

Area is always measured in **square units**, like in², cm² or m². You're measuring a flat surface, not a length.

## Example: a round garden pond

A pond is 10 meters across. How much surface area does the water have?

1. Diameter = 10 m, so radius = 10 ÷ 2 = 5 m
2. Square the radius: 5² = 25
3. Multiply by π: A = 25π ≈ 78.54
4. The pond has an area of about 78.54 m².

If you leave the answer as 25π m², that's the exact answer. The decimal is just rounded.

## Example: one big pizza or two small ones?

Back to the pizza deal. Is one 12-inch pizza more food than two 8-inch pizzas?

1. From above, one 12-inch pizza is about 113.10 in².
2. An 8-inch pizza has radius 4 in. Its area is π × 4² = 16π ≈ 50.27 in².
3. Two of them: 2 × 50.27 ≈ 100.53 in².
4. So the single 12-inch pizza is bigger, by about 12.57 in².

This surprises everyone the first time. 8 + 8 = 16, which is more than 12, so two small ones *feel* like more. But area depends on the radius **squared**, so a small bump in size makes a big jump in area.

## Why the formula actually works

Formulas stick better when you can see where they come from. Imagine cutting a circle into lots of thin slices, like a pizza, and laying them side by side, alternating which way they point. They form a shape that's almost a rectangle.

- Its height is the radius, r.
- Its width is half the distance around the circle. The full distance around is 2πr, so half of it is πr.

The area of a rectangle is width × height, which gives πr × r = πr². The thinner you cut the slices, the closer the shape gets to a perfect rectangle. That's the whole formula, hiding inside a rearranged pizza.

## Working backwards: finding the radius

If you know the area, you can find the radius by undoing each step in reverse. Suppose a circle has an area of 50 cm².

1. Divide by π: 50 ÷ π ≈ 15.92
2. Take the square root: √15.92 ≈ 3.99
3. The radius is about 3.99 cm.

Same formula, just run backwards. Divide first, square root second.

## Why this trips everyone up

I see the same four mistakes every single year. Learn them now and you'll skip a year of getting them wrong:

- **Using the diameter instead of the radius.** For the 12-inch pizza, plugging in 12 instead of 6 gives an answer four times too big. Always halve first.
- **Squaring π instead of r.** The formula is π × r × r, not π × π × r. Only the radius gets squared.
- **Mixing up area and circumference.** The distance around a circle is 2πr. The space inside it is πr². They look similar on paper and mean completely different things.
- **Forgetting square units.** An area answer should say cm², m² or similar. Plain "cm" means you measured a length, not a surface.

## Circumference vs area: don't mix them up

A circle has two numbers people constantly confuse: how far it is **around**, and how much space is **inside**.

- Circumference (the distance around) = 2πr
- Area (the space inside) = πr²

Here's my trick for telling them apart in a word problem. Look at the verbs:

- Words like "around", "fence", "border", "edge" or "track" mean circumference.
- Words like "covers", "fills", "inside", "surface" or "area" mean area.

The units give it away too. Circumference is a length, measured in plain units like meters. Area is measured in square units like m². If your "area" answer came out in plain meters, something went wrong.

Quick check with r = 5 m:

- Circumference = 2 × π × 5 ≈ 31.42 m
- Area = π × 5² = 25π ≈ 78.54 m²

Same circle, very different numbers. If your two answers look suspiciously similar, double-check which formula you grabbed.

## Where you'll actually use this

Circle area shows up more than you'd think:

- **Food deals.** Comparing pizza sizes tells you which is the better buy. Restaurants count on you not doing this math.
- **Gardens.** If one bag of grass seed covers 2 m² and your round flower bed is 12.57 m², you need 7 bags. You can't buy 6.285 bags, so always round up.
- **Paint.** A round table top of 1.54 m² needs that much paint per coat, times the number of coats.
- **Watering.** A sprinkler sprays in a circle of radius 4 m, so it waters π × 16 ≈ 50.27 m² of lawn.

## Extra worked example: area from a fence

A farmer puts up a circular fence that is 25 meters long. How much land does it enclose?

1. The fence is the circumference: 2πr = 25.
2. Find the radius: r = 25 ÷ (2π) ≈ 3.98 m.
3. Square the radius: 3.98² ≈ 15.84.
4. Multiply by π: A ≈ 15.84 × π ≈ 49.76 m².

So about 49.76 m² of land is enclosed. Notice we never needed the diameter. The radius came straight from the circumference.

## Mini FAQs

**What if I only know the circumference?**
You can still find the area. First find the radius: r = circumference ÷ (2π). A circle with a circumference of 20 m has a radius of 20 ÷ (2π) ≈ 3.18 m, so its area is 100/π ≈ 31.83 m².

**Can an area be negative?**
No. Area measures a real amount of space, so it's always zero or positive. A negative area means a sign error somewhere.

**Do I have to use the π button?**
Not always. Using 3.14 is fine for everyday questions, but school problems often want exact answers like 25π. When in doubt, give the exact answer first and the decimal after.
