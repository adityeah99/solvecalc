Fractions and decimals are two ways of writing the same amount. Half a sandwich is 1/2 of a sandwich, and it is also 0.5 of a sandwich.

The key idea is this: **a fraction is a division**. The line in the middle of 3/4 means "divided by". So 3/4 is the same as 3 ÷ 4, which equals 0.75.

## Method 1: Divide the top by the bottom

This method always works. Divide the numerator (top number) by the denominator (bottom number).

### Example: 3/4

1. Write it as a division: 3 ÷ 4
2. 4 does not go into 3, so write 0 and a decimal point, then think of 3 as 3.0
3. 4 goes into 30 seven times (7 × 4 = 28), with 2 left over.
4. Bring down another 0 to make 20. 4 goes into 20 five times, with nothing left.
5. So 3/4 = 0.75.

### Example: 3/8

1. Write it as a division: 3 ÷ 8
2. 8 goes into 30 three times (3 × 8 = 24), with 6 left over.
3. Bring down a 0 to make 60. 8 goes into 60 seven times (56), with 4 left over.
4. Bring down a 0 to make 40. 8 goes into 40 five times, with nothing left.
5. So 3/8 = 0.375.

On a calculator you would just type `3/8`.

## Method 2: Make the bottom 10, 100 or 1000

If the denominator can easily be turned into 10, 100 or 1000, you can skip the long division.

### Example: 7/20

1. What do you multiply 20 by to get 100? The answer is 5.
2. Multiply the top and bottom by 5: 7/20 = 35/100
3. 35 hundredths is written as 0.35.

This works well for denominators like 2, 4, 5, 20, 25 and 50. For example, 9/25 = 36/100 = 0.36.

## Decimals that repeat

Some fractions never finish when you divide. Try 1/3:

- 3 goes into 10 three times, with 1 left over.
- Bring down a 0 to make 10 again. 3 goes in three times, with 1 left over.
- This happens again and again forever.

So 1/3 = 0.333… where the 3 keeps going. This is called a **repeating decimal**. It is often written with a dot or a bar over the digit that repeats.

Here are a few more:

- 2/3 = 0.666…
- 5/6 = 0.8333… (only the 3 repeats)
- 2/11 = 0.1818… (the pair "18" repeats)

### How to tell if a decimal will stop

First simplify the fraction. Then look at the denominator. If the only prime numbers that divide it are 2 and 5, the decimal will stop. If any other prime divides it, such as 3, 7 or 11, the decimal will repeat.

- 3/8 stops, because 8 = 2 × 2 × 2.
- 7/20 stops, because 20 = 2 × 2 × 5.
- 5/6 repeats, because 6 = 2 × 3, and 3 is not 2 or 5.

## Mixed numbers

For a mixed number like 2 3/5, keep the whole number and convert only the fraction part.

1. Convert 3/5: 3 ÷ 5 = 0.6
2. Add the whole number: 2 + 0.6 = 2.6

## Common fractions worth knowing

| Fraction | Decimal | Percentage |
|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | 0.5 | 50% |
| 1/3 | 0.333… | 33.3…% |
| 1/4 | 0.25 | 25% |
| 3/4 | 0.75 | 75% |
| 1/5 | 0.2 | 20% |
| 1/8 | 0.125 | 12.5% |
| 1/10 | 0.1 | 10% |

To turn a decimal into a percentage, multiply by 100. So 0.375 becomes 37.5%. There is more on this in [how to calculate a percentage](/articles/how-to-calculate-a-percentage).

## Mistakes to watch for

- **Dividing the wrong way round.** 3/4 means 3 ÷ 4, not 4 ÷ 3. The top number goes first.
- **Reading the digits as a decimal.** 1/4 is not 1.4 or 0.14. It is 0.25.
- **Cutting a repeating decimal too short without saying so.** 1/3 is not exactly 0.33. If you round, use ≈ and say how many places, like 1/3 ≈ 0.33.
- **Changing only the bottom.** In Method 2, multiply the top and bottom by the same number.

## Try it yourself

1. Convert 5/8 to a decimal.
2. Convert 4/9 to a decimal.

**Answers:** 1) 5 ÷ 8 = 0.625. 2) 4 ÷ 9 = 0.444…, a repeating decimal.
