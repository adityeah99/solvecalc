Long division is a method for dividing larger numbers by hand, one digit at a time. It works for any division, even when the numbers are too big to do in your head.

The whole method is a short cycle repeated: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down. Once that rhythm clicks, long division becomes routine.

## The words you need

- **Dividend:** the number being divided (it goes inside).
- **Divisor:** the number you divide by (it goes outside).
- **Quotient:** the answer (it goes on top).
- **Remainder:** what is left over when the divisor does not fit exactly.

## The four-step cycle

For each digit, you repeat these steps:

1. **Divide:** how many times does the divisor fit?
2. **Multiply:** multiply that answer by the divisor.
3. **Subtract:** take it away to find what is left.
4. **Bring down:** carry down the next digit and start again.

## Worked example: 512 ÷ 16

Here the divisor is 16 and the dividend is 512.

1. Does 16 go into 5? No, because 16 is bigger than 5. Move on to the first two digits, 51.
2. Does 16 go into 51? Yes, 3 times, because 3 × 16 = 48. Write 3 in the quotient. Subtract: 51 − 48 = 3.
3. Bring down the next digit, 2, to make 32.
4. Does 16 go into 32? Yes, exactly 2 times, because 2 × 16 = 32. Write 2 in the quotient. Subtract: 32 − 32 = 0.

There is nothing left, so the answer is **32**. Check by multiplying back: 32 × 16 = 512.

## Worked example with a remainder: 953 ÷ 4

The divisor is 4 and the dividend is 953.

1. Does 4 go into 9? Yes, 2 times, because 2 × 4 = 8. Write 2. Subtract: 9 − 8 = 1.
2. Bring down the 5 to make 15. Does 4 go into 15? Yes, 3 times, because 3 × 4 = 12. Write 3. Subtract: 15 − 12 = 3.
3. Bring down the 3 to make 33. Does 4 go into 33? Yes, 8 times, because 8 × 4 = 32. Write 8. Subtract: 33 − 32 = 1.
4. There are no more digits to bring down, and 1 is left over.

So 953 ÷ 4 = **238 remainder 1**. Check: 238 × 4 = 952, and 952 + 1 = 953.

## Common mistakes

- **Skipping the "bring down" step.** After every subtraction, pull down the next digit before dividing again.
- **Misaligning the columns.** Keep each quotient digit above the digit you are working on, or the place values drift.
- **Choosing a quotient digit that is too big.** If your subtraction gives a number equal to or larger than the divisor, your digit was too small; if the multiply is bigger than the current number, it was too big.
- **Forgetting the remainder.** If nothing is left it is a clean division, but if there is a leftover, write it as the remainder.

## Long division vs short division

**Short division** is the compact version you use when the divisor is a single digit and you can hold the remainders in your head. For 864 ÷ 8, you work left to right: 8 into 8 is 1, 8 into 6 is 0 with 6 carried, making 64, and 8 into 64 is 8. Answer: 108. It is fast, but it only works while the mental juggling stays easy.

**Long division** writes every step down, which is why it handles bigger divisors like 16 or 27. The trade-off is space: long division takes more room on the page, but it never asks you to hold more than one small step in your head at a time.

Use short division for single-digit divisors you are comfortable with, and long division for everything else. They are the same mathematics, just different amounts of working shown.

## Worked example with a decimal answer: 875 ÷ 4

Sometimes the division does not come out even, and you want a decimal answer instead of a remainder.

1. Does 4 go into 8? Yes, 2 times, because 2 × 4 = 8. Write 2. Subtract: 8 − 8 = 0.
2. Bring down the 7. Does 4 go into 7? Yes, 1 time, because 1 × 4 = 4. Write 1. Subtract: 7 − 4 = 3.
3. Bring down the 5 to make 35. Does 4 go into 35? Yes, 8 times, because 8 × 4 = 32. Write 8. Subtract: 35 − 32 = 3.
4. There are no more digits, but 3 is left over. Write a decimal point in the quotient and add a 0 to the dividend, making 30.
5. Does 4 go into 30? Yes, 7 times, because 7 × 4 = 28. Write 7 after the point. Subtract: 30 − 28 = 2.
6. Add another 0, making 20. Does 4 go into 20? Yes, exactly 5 times. Write 5. Subtract: 20 − 20 = 0.

So 875 ÷ 4 = **218.75**. Check: 218.75 × 4 = 875.

## Quick reference: the cycle

| Step | What to do | Ask yourself |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Divide | See how many times the divisor fits | "Does it fit? How many times?" |
| 2. Multiply | Multiply that digit by the divisor | "What is 3 × 16?" |
| 3. Subtract | Take it away from the working number | "What is left?" |
| 4. Bring down | Carry down the next digit | "Is there another digit?" |

Repeat until there are no digits left. If something is still left over, it is the remainder, or keep going past a decimal point for a decimal answer.

## Where long division shows up

- **Splitting costs.** Four friends share a $875 holiday cabin. 875 ÷ 4 = 218.75, so each pays $218.75.
- **Time.** A 953-minute playlist is 953 ÷ 60 hours. That works out to 15 hours with 53 minutes left over.
- **Batches.** You have 512 stickers and packs hold 16. 512 ÷ 16 = 32 packs exactly, no leftovers.

## Quick questions

**What if the divisor is bigger than the first digit?**

Just start with the first two digits, like the 512 ÷ 16 example did with 51. If the divisor is bigger than the first two digits as well, which can happen with three-digit divisors, start with the first three.

**Can I check a long division answer on a calculator?**

Yes, and you should while learning. Divide on the calculator and compare. If the calculator shows a decimal like 238.25 and your hand working gave 238 remainder 1, check: 0.25 × 4 = 1, so they match.

## Try it yourself

1. Work out 864 ÷ 8.
2. Work out 275 ÷ 6.

**Answers:** 1) 8 goes into 86 ten times (80), leaving 6; bring down the 4 to make 64; 8 goes into 64 eight times, so the answer is **108**. Check: 108 × 8 = 864. 2) 6 into 27 is 4 (24), leaving 3; bring down the 5 to make 35; 6 into 35 is 5 (30), leaving 5, so the answer is **45 remainder 5**. Check: 45 × 6 = 270, plus 5 is 275.
