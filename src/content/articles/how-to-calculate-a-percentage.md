Percentages are a way to describe a part of 100. For example, 25% means 25 out of 100. If something costs $80 and it is 25% off, the discount is $20.

You will see percentages on sale signs, test results, phone batteries and weather apps. The good news is that almost every percentage question is one of three types, and each one has a simple method.

## What "percent" really means

The word "percent" comes from "per hundred". So 40% is the same as 40 out of 100, which is the fraction 40/100 or the decimal 0.40.

To turn a percentage into a decimal, divide it by 100. That just means moving the decimal point two places to the left:

- 25% = 0.25
- 7% = 0.07
- 150% = 1.5

This decimal form is the one you will use most when you calculate.

## Type 1: Finding a percentage of a number

This is the most common question. "What is 15% of 200?"

The method is: change the percentage to a decimal, then multiply.

### Example: 15% of 200

1. Change 15% to a decimal: 15 ÷ 100 = 0.15
2. Multiply: 0.15 × 200 = 30
3. So 15% of 200 is 30.

On a calculator you could type `200*15%` or `0.15*200`.

### Example: a phone on sale

A phone costs $80 and the shop takes 25% off. How much do you pay?

1. Change 25% to a decimal: 0.25
2. Find the discount: 0.25 × 80 = 20. The discount is $20.
3. Subtract from the price: 80 − 20 = 60. You pay $60.

A quicker way is to notice that if 25% is taken off, you pay the other 75%. So 0.75 × 80 = 60, which gives the same answer in one step.

## Type 2: What percent is one number of another?

Here you know the part and the whole, and you want the percentage. The method is: divide the part by the whole, then multiply by 100.

### Example: a test score

You got 18 out of 24 on a science test. What is your score as a percentage?

1. Divide the part by the whole: 18 ÷ 24 = 0.75
2. Multiply by 100: 0.75 × 100 = 75
3. Your score is 75%.

The "whole" is always the total, the thing you are comparing against. In this case it is the 24 marks available.

## Type 3: Finding the whole from a part

Sometimes you know the part and the percentage, but not the total. For example: "12 students walk to school. That is 30% of the class. How many students are in the class?"

The method is: divide the part by the percentage written as a decimal.

### Example: the size of a class

1. Change 30% to a decimal: 0.30
2. Divide the part by it: 12 ÷ 0.30 = 40
3. There are 40 students in the class.

Check it: 30% of 40 is 0.30 × 40 = 12. That matches.

## Quick tricks for mental math

You can do many percentages in your head if you start from 10%.

- **10%**: divide by 10. 10% of 350 is 35.
- **5%**: half of 10%. 5% of 350 is 17.5.
- **20%**: double 10%. 20% of 350 is 70.
- **50%**: divide by 2. 50% of 350 is 175.

Want a 15% tip on a $40 meal? 10% is $4 and 5% is $2, so 15% is $6.

## Mistakes to watch for

- **Forgetting to divide by 100.** 15% of 200 is not 15 × 200 = 3000. Always turn 15% into 0.15 first.
- **Dividing the wrong way round.** For "what percent is 18 of 24", divide 18 ÷ 24, not 24 ÷ 18. The part goes on top.
- **Using the wrong whole.** If a question says "30% of the class", the class is the whole, not the group you already counted.
- **Stopping at the discount.** If a question asks what you pay, subtract the discount from the original price.

## Try it yourself

1. What is 35% of 60?
2. You scored 27 out of 30 on a quiz. What percentage is that?

**Answers:** 1) 0.35 × 60 = 21. 2) 27 ÷ 30 = 0.9, so 90%.
