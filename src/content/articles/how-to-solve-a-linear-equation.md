Solving an equation means finding the value of the letter that makes both sides equal. For 3x − 5 = 16, you want to find the number x that makes the left side come out to 16.

The whole method rests on one idea: an equation is like a balance scale. If you do the same thing to both sides, it stays balanced.

## The balance rule

You are allowed to:

- add the same number to both sides
- subtract the same number from both sides
- multiply both sides by the same number
- divide both sides by the same number (as long as it is not zero)

Your goal is to get the letter on its own on one side. To do that, you undo whatever is being done to it, in reverse order.

## Example 1: a one-step equation

Solve x + 7 = 15.

1. Something is being added to x: the 7. Undo it by subtracting 7 from both sides.
2. x + 7 − 7 = 15 − 7
3. x = 8

**Check:** 8 + 7 = 15. Correct.

## Example 2: a two-step equation

Solve 3x − 5 = 16.

Here x is multiplied by 3, then 5 is taken away. Undo these in reverse order: deal with the − 5 first, then the × 3.

1. Add 5 to both sides: 3x = 21
2. Divide both sides by 3: x = 7

**Check:** 3 × 7 − 5 = 21 − 5 = 16. Correct.

## Example 3: letters on both sides

Solve 5x + 2 = 2x + 14.

When x appears on both sides, first gather all the x terms on one side.

1. Subtract 2x from both sides: 3x + 2 = 14
2. Subtract 2 from both sides: 3x = 12
3. Divide both sides by 3: x = 4

**Check:** Left side is 5 × 4 + 2 = 22. Right side is 2 × 4 + 14 = 22. Both match.

It is usually easiest to move the smaller x term, so that you are left with a positive number of x's.

## Example 4: an equation with brackets

Solve 4(x − 3) = 20.

1. Multiply out the bracket. The 4 multiplies both parts: 4x − 12 = 20
2. Add 12 to both sides: 4x = 32
3. Divide both sides by 4: x = 8

**Check:** 4 × (8 − 3) = 4 × 5 = 20. Correct.

There is a shortcut here too. Since everything on the left is multiplied by 4, you could divide both sides by 4 first: x − 3 = 5, so x = 8.

## Example 5: an equation with a fraction

Solve x/3 + 2 = 6.

1. Subtract 2 from both sides: x/3 = 4
2. x is being divided by 3, so multiply both sides by 3: x = 12

**Check:** 12 ÷ 3 + 2 = 4 + 2 = 6. Correct.

## Using equations for real problems

Three friends go to the movies. They buy three tickets and share one popcorn that costs $6. The total is $36. How much is one ticket?

1. Let t be the price of one ticket. Three tickets plus popcorn is 3t + 6.
2. Write the equation: 3t + 6 = 36
3. Subtract 6 from both sides: 3t = 30
4. Divide by 3: t = 10
5. Each ticket costs $10.

## Negative answers are fine

Solve −2x = 10. Divide both sides by −2 and you get x = −5. Check: −2 × −5 = 10. A negative number times a negative number is positive, so this works.

## Mistakes to watch for

- **Doing something to only one side.** If you subtract 7 on the left, you must subtract 7 on the right too.
- **Sign slips.** When you move a term across, it changes from + to − or − to +. It helps to write the step in full, like "subtract 2x from both sides", rather than jumping ahead.
- **Only multiplying the first part of a bracket.** 4(x − 3) is 4x − 12, not 4x − 3.
- **Skipping the check.** Putting your answer back into the original equation takes a few seconds and catches most mistakes.

## Try it yourself

1. Solve 6x + 4 = 40.
2. Solve 2(x + 5) = 3x + 1.

**Answers:** 1) 6x = 36, so x = 6. 2) 2x + 10 = 3x + 1. Subtract 2x: 10 = x + 1, so x = 9. Check: 2 × 14 = 28 and 3 × 9 + 1 = 28.
