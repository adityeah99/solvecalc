The perimeter of a shape is the total distance around its edge. If you walked all the way around a field, the distance you walked would be its perimeter.

You need perimeter whenever you are measuring something that goes around the outside: a fence for a garden, a frame for a picture, or a strip of trim around a room.

## The formula

A rectangle has four sides. The two long sides are the same length, and the two short sides are the same length. We usually call these the **length** (l) and the **width** (w).

To find the perimeter, add all four sides:

**P = l + w + l + w**

Since there are two of each, this is the same as:

**P = 2 × (l + w)**

Both give the same answer. The second one is just quicker.

## Example: fencing a garden

A rectangular vegetable garden is 8 meters long and 5 meters wide. How much fencing do you need to go all the way around?

1. Add the length and width: 8 + 5 = 13
2. Double it: 2 × 13 = 26
3. You need 26 meters of fencing.

You can check by adding all four sides: 8 + 5 + 8 + 5 = 26. Same answer.

## Example: trim around a bedroom

A bedroom floor is 4.5 m long and 3.2 m wide. How much skirting board goes around the edge (ignoring the door)?

1. Add the length and width: 4.5 + 3.2 = 7.7
2. Double it: 2 × 7.7 = 15.4
3. You need 15.4 m of skirting board.

Decimals work exactly the same way as whole numbers here.

## Example: sides in different units

A poster is 2 m tall and 75 cm wide. What is its perimeter?

You cannot add meters and centimeters directly. First, put both sides in the same unit.

1. Change 2 m into centimeters: 2 × 100 = 200 cm
2. Add the length and width: 200 + 75 = 275
3. Double it: 2 × 275 = 550
4. The perimeter is 550 cm, which is the same as 5.5 m.

## Squares are a special case

A square is a rectangle with all four sides equal. If each side is s, then:

**P = 4 × s**

A square tile with sides of 30 cm has a perimeter of 4 × 30 = 120 cm.

## Working backwards: finding a missing side

Sometimes you know the perimeter and one side, and you need the other side.

A rectangular dog pen has a perimeter of 36 m. It is 11 m long. How wide is it?

1. Halve the perimeter. This gives one length plus one width: 36 ÷ 2 = 18
2. Take away the length: 18 − 11 = 7
3. The pen is 7 m wide.

Check: 2 × (11 + 7) = 2 × 18 = 36. Correct.

## Perimeter is not area

Perimeter measures the edge. Area measures the space inside. They are different things, and two rectangles with the same perimeter can have very different areas.

| Rectangle | Perimeter | Area |
|---|---|---|
| 6 m by 4 m | 2 × (6 + 4) = 20 m | 6 × 4 = 24 m² |
| 9 m by 1 m | 2 × (9 + 1) = 20 m | 9 × 1 = 9 m² |

Both use 20 m of fence, but the first one holds far more space. Notice the units too. Perimeter is a length, so it is in m. Area is in square units, m².

If you want to see how area works for a curved shape, read [how to find the area of a circle](/articles/area-of-a-circle).

## Mistakes to watch for

- **Adding only two sides.** 8 + 5 = 13 is only half the way around. Remember to double it.
- **Mixing units.** Change everything to the same unit before adding.
- **Multiplying instead of adding.** 8 × 5 gives the area, not the perimeter.
- **Writing square units.** Perimeter is a distance, so write m or cm, not m² or cm².

## Quick reference: perimeters of common shapes

The rectangle is not the only shape with a perimeter. Here are the formulas you will meet most often:

| Shape | Perimeter formula | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Square | P = 4 × s | four equal sides |
| Rectangle | P = 2 × (l + w) | twice length plus width |
| Triangle | P = a + b + c | add all three sides |
| Circle | P = 2 × π × r | called the **circumference** |

The idea is always the same: add up every side. The circle is the odd one out, because it has no straight sides, so we use π (about 3.14) instead.

## Example: fencing with a gate

A rectangular garden is 12 m long and 9 m wide. You want to fence it all the way around, but leave a 1.5 m gap for the gate. How much fencing do you need?

1. Find the full perimeter: 2 × (12 + 9) = 2 × 21 = 42 m.
2. Take away the gate gap: 42 − 1.5 = 40.5 m.
3. You need 40.5 m of fencing.

Real projects almost always have a gap like this: a door in the skirting board, or a driveway opening in a fence. Work out the full perimeter first, then subtract the gaps.

## When you double the sides

Doubling the sides of a rectangle doubles the perimeter. But watch what happens to the area:

| Rectangle | Perimeter | Area |
|---|---|---|
| 4 m by 6 m | 2 × (4 + 6) = 20 m | 4 × 6 = 24 m² |
| 8 m by 12 m | 2 × (8 + 12) = 40 m | 8 × 12 = 96 m² |

The perimeter doubled from 20 to 40, but the area went from 24 to 96, which is four times bigger. Perimeter grows at the same rate as the sides. Area grows with the square of the sides. This is why a slightly bigger pizza gives you much more food for only a little more crust.

## Quick questions

**Do I have to use the same units everywhere?**

Yes. You cannot add 2 m and 75 cm directly. Convert everything to one unit first, then add, as in the poster example above.

**Can two shapes have the same perimeter but different areas?**

Yes. Earlier in this article, a 6-by-4 and a 9-by-1 rectangle both have a perimeter of 20 m, but areas of 24 m² and 9 m². Of all rectangles with a fixed perimeter, the square holds the most area.

**Is the perimeter of a circle called something special?**

It is called the circumference. The formula is C = 2 × π × r, where r is the radius. For a circle with radius 5 cm, the circumference is about 2 × 3.14 × 5 = 31.4 cm.

## Try it yourself

1. Find the perimeter of a rectangle that is 12 cm long and 7 cm wide.
2. A rectangle has a perimeter of 50 m and a width of 10 m. How long is it?

**Answers:** 1) 2 × (12 + 7) = 2 × 19 = 38 cm. 2) 50 ÷ 2 = 25, then 25 − 10 = 15 m.
