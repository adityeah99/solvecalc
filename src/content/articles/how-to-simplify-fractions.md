Simplifying a fraction means writing it with smaller numbers without changing what it's worth. 4/8 and 1/2 are the same amount of cake. If you cut a cake into 8 slices and take 4, you've got half the cake. The 1/2 version is just easier to think about, and that's the whole point.

A fraction is in its **simplest form** when the top and bottom have no common factor except 1. No whole number bigger than 1 divides into both.

## The main idea

To simplify, divide the top (numerator) and the bottom (denominator) by the same number. Same treatment for both, value stays the same. That's the one rule that never changes.

The fastest route is dividing by the **greatest common divisor** (GCD): the biggest number that divides into both. Some books call it the highest common factor, or HCF. Same thing, different name. Don't let the jargon bother you.

## Finding the GCD without tears

The straightforward way: list the factors of each number and find the biggest one they share.

For 18 and 24:

- Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18
- Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24
- Shared: 1, 2, 3, 6
- Biggest shared factor is 6, so the GCD is 6.

Yes, listing factors feels slow. It is slow. But it always works, and while you're learning, "always works" beats "fast but I might mess it up".

## Example: simplify 18/24

1. Find the GCD of 18 and 24. From the lists above, it's 6.
2. Divide the top by 6: 18 ÷ 6 = 3
3. Divide the bottom by 6: 24 ÷ 6 = 4
4. The answer is 3/4.

Can 3/4 go any further? The only number dividing into both 3 and 4 is 1, so no. Done. Simplest form.

## Example: simplify 45/75

Say you answered 45 out of 75 questions correctly across a term of homework. As a fraction, that's 45/75. Ugly. Let's fix it.

1. Factors of 45: 1, 3, 5, 9, 15, 45
2. Factors of 75: 1, 3, 5, 15, 25, 75
3. The biggest shared factor is 15.
4. Divide both by 15: 45 ÷ 15 = 3 and 75 ÷ 15 = 5
5. The answer is 3/5.

So you got 3 out of every 5 right. See how much easier that is to picture than 45 out of 75?

## The lazy method (I mean that as a compliment)

Can't spot the GCD? No problem. Just keep dividing by small numbers that go into both, like 2, 3 or 5, until you can't go further. I call this the lazy method, and honestly it's what most people actually do.

### Example: simplify 48/72

1. Both are even, so divide by 2: 24/36
2. Still both even, divide by 2 again: 12/18
3. Still both even, divide by 2 again: 6/9
4. Both divide by 3: 2/3
5. 2 and 3 share nothing except 1, so stop. The answer is 2/3.

Four steps instead of one. If you'd known the GCD of 48 and 72 is 24, it'd be one step: 48 ÷ 24 = 2 and 72 ÷ 24 = 3. Both roads reach 2/3. Use whichever you trust more.

## Quick tests for common factors

These little checks help you spot something to divide by:

- **2**: both numbers are even.
- **3**: the digits of each number add up to a multiple of 3. For 45, 4 + 5 = 9, so 3 divides 45.
- **5**: both numbers end in 0 or 5.
- **10**: both numbers end in 0.

The digit-sum trick for 3 surprises students every time. Try it on 123: 1 + 2 + 3 = 6, and sure enough, 123 ÷ 3 = 41.

## What about fractions bigger than 1?

Same method, no new rules. Take 20/8:

1. The GCD of 20 and 8 is 4.
2. 20 ÷ 4 = 5 and 8 ÷ 4 = 2, so 20/8 = 5/2
3. As a mixed number, 5/2 = 2 1/2.

Top-heavy fractions simplify exactly like normal ones. The top being bigger changes nothing.

## Mistakes I see every year

- **Dividing only one number.** Divide the top by 6 but forget the bottom, and you've changed the value completely. Both. Always both.
- **Subtracting instead of dividing.** Taking 2 off the top and bottom of 4/6 gives 2/4, which is a different amount. I don't know why so many students try this, but they do. Always divide.
- **Stopping too early.** 12/18 is simpler than 48/72, but it's not simplest form. Keep going until the only common factor is 1.
- **Trying to simplify across a plus sign.** You simplify a single fraction, not pieces of a sum. Add first, then simplify the answer.

## Simplifying vs equivalent fractions

These two get tangled up in students' heads, so let's untangle them.

**Equivalent fractions** are different-looking fractions with the same value: 1/2, 2/4, 3/6 and 50/100 are all equivalent. You make them by multiplying or dividing top and bottom by the same number.

**Simplifying** is the process of finding the simplest equivalent fraction, the one with the smallest numbers. It's one direction: from 50/100 down to 1/2.

They're partners, really. When you add fractions with different denominators, you go the other way first: turn both into equivalent fractions with a bigger shared denominator, add them, then simplify the answer back down. One skill fattens fractions up to match them; the other slims the answer down at the end.

## Quick reference: divisibility tests

| Divide by | Quick test | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Both numbers are even | 36 and 60 are both even |
| 3 | The digits of each number add to a multiple of 3 | 36: 3 + 6 = 9, so 3 divides 36 |
| 4 | The last two digits form a multiple of 4 | 36: 36 ÷ 4 = 9 |
| 5 | Both numbers end in 0 or 5 | 45 and 75 both end in 5 |
| 6 | Both tests for 2 and 3 pass | 36 and 60 are even and their digits sum to multiples of 3 |
| 10 | Both numbers end in 0 | 30 and 40 both end in 0 |

## Example: simplify 36/60

1. List the factors of 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36
2. List the factors of 60: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20, 30, 60
3. The biggest shared factor is 12.
4. Divide both by 12: 36 ÷ 12 = 3 and 60 ÷ 12 = 5
5. The answer is 3/5.

Check: 3/5 = 0.6 and 36/60 = 0.6. Same value, smaller numbers.

Didn't spot 12? The lazy method works fine: divide by 2 to get 18/30, by 2 again to get 9/15, then by 3 to get 3/5.

## Where simplifying actually shows up

- **Cooking.** A recipe calls for 3/4 of a cup and you want to halve it. Half of 3/4 is 3/8, already simple. But doubling gives 6/4, which simplifies to 3/2, or 1 1/2 cups. Nobody measures "6/4 cups".
- **Test scores.** 18 out of 24 right is 18/24, which is 3/4, or 75%. Teachers and students both prefer the simple version. It just reads faster.
- **Discounts.** A $20 saving on an $80 item is 20/80 = 1/4, a quarter off. The simplified fraction tells you the deal at a glance.
