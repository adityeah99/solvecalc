A fraction tells you how many equal pieces you have out of a whole. In 3/8, the bottom number (the denominator) says the whole is cut into 8 pieces, and the top number (the numerator) says you have 3 of them.

Adding fractions is easy when the pieces are the same size. When they are not, you first cut them into pieces that match. That is the whole idea.

## Adding fractions with the same denominator

Imagine a pizza cut into 8 equal slices. You eat 2 slices and your friend eats 3. Together you ate 2/8 + 3/8 of the pizza.

The slices are all the same size, so you just count them:

1. Check the denominators. Both are 8, so they match.
2. Add the numerators: 2 + 3 = 5
3. Keep the denominator the same: 5/8

Together you ate 5/8 of the pizza. The denominator stays at 8 because the slices are still eighths. You never add the bottom numbers.

## Adding fractions with different denominators

Now try 1/3 + 1/4. A third and a quarter are different sized pieces, so you cannot just count them. You need a common denominator: a number that both 3 and 4 divide into.

The best choice is the **least common multiple** (LCM) of the denominators. The multiples of 3 are 3, 6, 9, 12, 15… and the multiples of 4 are 4, 8, 12, 16… The first number on both lists is 12.

### Example: 1/3 + 1/4

1. Find the LCM of 3 and 4: it is 12.
2. Rewrite 1/3 with denominator 12. Since 3 × 4 = 12, multiply top and bottom by 4: 1/3 = 4/12
3. Rewrite 1/4 with denominator 12. Since 4 × 3 = 12, multiply top and bottom by 3: 1/4 = 3/12
4. Add the numerators: 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12

So 1/3 + 1/4 = 7/12.

Multiplying the top and bottom by the same number does not change the value of a fraction. 4/12 is exactly the same amount as 1/3, just cut into smaller pieces.

### Example: 5/6 + 3/4

1. Find the LCM of 6 and 4. Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18… Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12… The LCM is 12.
2. Rewrite 5/6: multiply top and bottom by 2 to get 10/12
3. Rewrite 3/4: multiply top and bottom by 3 to get 9/12
4. Add: 10/12 + 9/12 = 19/12
5. The top is bigger than the bottom, so you can write it as a mixed number. 12 goes into 19 once with 7 left over, so 19/12 = 1 7/12.

## Adding mixed numbers

A mixed number, like 2 1/2, has a whole-number part and a fraction part. One easy way to add them is to add the wholes and the fractions separately.

### Example: 2 1/2 + 1 2/3

1. Add the whole numbers: 2 + 1 = 3
2. Add the fractions: 1/2 + 2/3. The LCM of 2 and 3 is 6, so this is 3/6 + 4/6 = 7/6
3. 7/6 is more than one whole. It equals 1 1/6.
4. Add everything: 3 + 1 1/6 = 4 1/6

So 2 1/2 + 1 2/3 = 4 1/6.

## Simplify at the end

After adding, check if your answer can be simplified. For example:

- 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/6 + 2/6 = 3/6
- 3 and 6 can both be divided by 3, so 3/6 = 1/2

If you want more practice with this step, read [how to simplify fractions](/articles/how-to-simplify-fractions).

## Mistakes to watch for

- **Adding the denominators.** 1/2 + 1/3 is not 2/5. Half a pizza plus a third of a pizza is clearly more than half, but 2/5 is less than half. The right answer is 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.
- **Changing only the bottom number.** When you turn 1/3 into twelfths, the top must change too. 1/3 = 4/12, not 1/12.
- **Using a common denominator that is not common.** Make sure every denominator divides into the one you choose.
- **Forgetting to simplify.** 3/6 is correct, but 1/2 is the neater final answer.

## Try it yourself

1. 2/5 + 1/10
2. 3/4 + 2/3

**Answers:** 1) 4/10 + 1/10 = 5/10, which simplifies to 1/2. 2) 9/12 + 8/12 = 17/12, which is 1 5/12.
