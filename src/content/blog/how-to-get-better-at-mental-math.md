Nobody is born good at mental math. I say this because I believed the opposite for years, and it held me back. The kids who calculate fast are not geniuses. They are people who learned a handful of moves and practiced them until the moves became reflexes. You can do the same thing. Here is the plan.

## The core idea: break numbers apart

Almost every mental math trick is the same idea wearing different clothes: **break a hard calculation into easy pieces, then put them back together.**

47 + 36 looks annoying. But 47 + 30 = 77, and 77 + 6 = 83. Two easy steps instead of one hard one. That is the whole secret. The rest of this post is just variations on that move, and a plan to make it automatic.

## The five moves worth learning

**1. Make friendly numbers.** Round one number to something easy, then fix the difference. 98 + 47 becomes 100 + 47 = 147, minus the 2 you added, giving 145. Your brain loves round numbers. Give it round numbers.

**2. Break multiplication apart.** 7 × 48 looks hard. But 7 × 50 = 350, minus 7 × 2 = 14, gives 336. Or go the other way: 7 × 40 = 280 plus 7 × 8 = 56, total 336. Either path works. Pick whichever feels easier in the moment.

**3. Halve and double.** Multiplying by 5? Halve the number and add a zero: 5 × 84 becomes 42 with a zero, 420. Multiplying by 50? Halve it and add two zeros. Dividing by 5? Double it and drop a zero. These feel like cheating. They are just arithmetic.

**4. Left-to-right addition.** Forget the column method you learned on paper. For mental math, go left to right: 456 + 378 becomes 400 + 300 = 700, then 56 + 78 = 134, total 834. Working left to right keeps the big digits in your head first, which is where your attention is strongest.

**5. Know your anchors.** 25% is a quarter. 10% is divide by 10. Squares up to 15² show up constantly: 12² = 144, 13² = 169, 15² = 225. The [times tables guide](/articles/times-tables) will get your multiplication facts solid, and everything else in mental math is built on those facts.

## The daily ten-minute drill

Here is the part most advice skips: the actual routine. Talent is just practice that happened daily. Do this for ten minutes a day and you will feel the difference within two weeks.

**Minutes 1 to 3: warm-up.** Simple addition and subtraction, left to right. 47 + 36. 83 - 29. 156 + 244. Easy wins to get your brain in gear.

**Minutes 4 to 7: one move.** Pick one of the five moves above and drill only that. Today: friendly numbers. 10 problems, all friendly numbers. Tomorrow: halve and double. Rotating one move per day keeps it fresh and builds each skill separately.

**Minutes 8 to 10: mixed challenge.** Random problems, any type, slightly harder than comfortable. This is where the moves start combining on their own, which is the whole point.

That is it. Ten minutes. I have students do this while waiting for the bus. Consistency beats intensity here: ten minutes daily will destroy one hour weekly, every time.

## The mistakes that slow everyone down

- **Trying to visualize the column method.** The paper algorithm is designed for paper, not heads. Let it go. Left-to-right is the mental method.
- **Going too fast too soon.** Speed is a *result* of accuracy, not a shortcut to it. Get it right slowly first. Speed shows up on its own around week three.
- **Practicing only easy numbers.** If 47 + 36 is comfortable, it is not practice anymore. Push slightly past comfortable. That is where growth lives.

## Where this pays off

Mental math is not a party trick. It is a thinking tool. When you can estimate 15% of a bill in seconds, you catch the cashier's error. When you can sanity-check a calculation in your head, you stop trusting wrong calculator outputs. Every estimation you do makes you harder to fool, by machines and by people. That is worth ten minutes a day.
