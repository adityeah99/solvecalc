Doing math in your head isn't a magic gift. Nobody is born with it. It's a handful of patterns that let you take a shortcut instead of the long route, and once you see how each one works, you'll reach for them without thinking.

I use these constantly. Here they're, each with a real example.

## The 11 trick: add the digits, drop the sum in the middle

To multiply a two-digit number by 11, add its two digits and slide the sum between them.

Take 25 × 11. The digits are 2 and 5, and 2 + 5 = 7, so the answer is **275**. That's it. One addition.

When the middle sum is 10 or more, carry the extra to the left. For 47 × 11: 4 + 7 = 11. Write the 1, carry the other 1 to the 4, which becomes 5: **517**. Why does it work? Because 11 is just 10 + 1, so you're adding the number to itself shifted one place over. Neat, right?

## Squaring numbers that end in 5: stick 25 on the end

Any number ending in 5 squares in one quick move. Take the tens digit, multiply it by the next number up, and stick 25 on the end.

For 35²: the tens digit is 3, the next number is 4, so 3 × 4 = 12. Add 25: **1225**. For 65²: 6 × 7 = 42, then 25: **4225**. Fast, exact, and it makes you look like a genius at dinner parties. Not that I would know.

## Doubling and halving: trade until it's easy

If one number in a multiplication is even, halve it and double the other. You keep trading until one number is round enough to handle in your head.

16 × 25 looks awkward. Halve and double: 8 × 50. Again: 4 × 100 = **400**. Done. The problem just melts.

## Multiplying by 5: go through 10

To multiply by 5, multiply by 10 and take half, since 5 is half of 10. Obvious once you see it, invisible until then.

48 × 5: ten times 48 is 480, half of that's **240**. Going the other way works too when the number is odd.

## Percentages: anchor on 10%

Percentages get easy once you anchor on 10%. To find 10% of anything, just move the decimal one place left.

Want a 15% tip on a $40 bill? 10% of 40 is 4. Half of that, 5%, is 2. Add them: 4 + 2 = **$6**. Need 20%? That's just 10% doubled. Break any percentage into 10s, 5s, and 1s, and it becomes a small pile of easy pieces. I do this in restaurants without thinking now.

## Ugly addition: round first, fix it after

Ugly sums get friendly when you round to a tidy number first, then adjust.

47 + 38: round 38 up to 40. Now 47 + 40 = 87, which is easy. You added 2 too many, so subtract them back: **85**. Rounding turns the hard part into something you can actually picture in your head.

## One warning: order still matters

Mental tricks are only as good as the order you apply them in. When a problem mixes operations, the usual rules still hold: multiply and divide before you add and subtract. No trick overrides that.

So 2 + 3 × 4 is 2 + 12 = 14, not 20. If that ever feels slippery, the [order of operations article](/articles/order-of-operations) lays it out step by step.

## The unsexy foundation

Here's the honest part: none of this replaces knowing your [times tables](/articles/times-tables) cold. The tricks are shortcuts *around* the facts, and they run smoothest when the basic facts are automatic. There's no trick for not knowing 7 × 8.

Practice one trick at a time until it feels natural, then add the next. Within a couple of weeks you'll be surprised how much you can do without reaching for a pencil.
