Doing math in your head is not a magic gift. It is a handful of patterns that let you take a shortcut instead of the long route. Once you see how each one works, you will reach for them without thinking.

Here are the ones I use most, each with a real example.

## Multiplying a two-digit number by 11

To multiply a two-digit number by 11, add its two digits and drop the sum in the middle.

Take 25 × 11. The digits are 2 and 5, and 2 + 5 = 7, so slide the 7 between them: **275**.

When the middle sum is 10 or more, carry the extra to the left. For 47 × 11, we get 4 + 7 = 11. Write the 1, and carry the other 1 to the 4, which becomes 5: **517**. It works because 11 is just 10 + 1, so you are adding the number to itself shifted one place over.

## Squaring a number that ends in 5

Any number ending in 5 squares in one quick move. Take the tens digit, multiply it by the next number up, and stick 25 on the end.

For 35², the tens digit is 3 and the next number is 4, so 3 × 4 = 12. Add 25: **1225**. For 65², use 6 × 7 = 42, then 25: **4225**. It is fast and always exact.

## Doubling and halving

If one number in a multiplication is even, you can halve it and double the other to reach an easier problem.

Say 16 × 25 looks awkward. Halve and double: 8 × 50, and again 4 × 100 = **400**. You keep swapping until one number is round enough to handle in your head.

## Multiplying by 5

To multiply by 5, multiply by 10 and take half, since 5 is half of 10.

For 48 × 5, ten times 48 is 480, and half of that is **240**. Going the other way works too when the number is odd.

## Fast percentages

Percentages get easy once you anchor on 10%. To find 10% of anything, move the decimal one place left.

Say you want a 15% tip on a $40 bill. 10% of 40 is 4. Half of that, 5%, is 2. Add them: 4 + 2 = **$6**. Need 20%? That is just 10% doubled. Break any percentage into 10s, 5s, and 1s, and it becomes a small pile of easy pieces.

For anything with messier numbers, the [percentage calculator](/calculators/percentage) checks your work in a tap.

## Adding by rounding, then adjusting

Ugly sums get friendly when you round to a tidy number first.

For 47 + 38, round 38 up to 40. Now 47 + 40 = 87. You added 2 too many, so subtract them back: **85**. Rounding turns the hard part into something you can picture.

## A word of caution about order

Mental tricks are only as good as the order you apply them in. When a problem mixes operations, the usual rules still hold: multiply and divide before you add and subtract.

So 2 + 3 × 4 is 2 + 12 = 14, not 20. If that ever feels slippery, the [order of operations article](/articles/order-of-operations) lays it out step by step.

## The habit that makes it stick

None of this replaces knowing your [times tables](/articles/times-tables) cold. The tricks are shortcuts *around* the facts, and they run smoothest when the basic facts are automatic.

Practice one trick at a time until it feels natural, then add the next. Within a couple of weeks you will be surprised how much you can do without reaching for a pencil.
