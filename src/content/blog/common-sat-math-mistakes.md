I have graded enough practice SATs to predict the score before I finish the first section. Not because I am psychic. Because the same five mistakes show up on almost every paper, and they are all fixable. If you are prepping for the SAT, fixing these five is worth more than learning any new topic.

## Mistake 1: answering the wrong question

This is the number one point-killer, and it has nothing to do with math ability.

The question asks for the value of 2x. You solve for x, feel great, bubble in x, and move on. Wrong. The test wanted 2x. Or it asks "which of the following is NOT true" and you pick the first true statement you see. Or it gives you the radius and asks for the diameter.

The fix is embarrassingly simple: **before you start calculating, underline what the question actually asks for.** Then, after you finish, read the question one more time and check that your answer matches what was asked. Ten seconds. I have watched this single habit save students 30 to 50 points.

## Mistake 2: calculator mode and entry errors

The SAT lets you use a calculator, which means the SAT also lets you make calculator mistakes. The classics:

- Typing `200*15%` when you meant something else entirely.
- Forgetting your calculator is in radian mode when the question uses degrees. I have watched this cost students real marks on actual tests. Check the mode. Every time.
- Trusting the display without sanity-checking. If your answer says a pizza costs $4,000, you mis-keyed something.

The fix: treat the calculator as a fast but dumb assistant. Estimate the answer in your head first, then check the calculator agrees roughly. And learn your calculator *before* test day. The week of the test is not the time to discover where the fraction button is. Practice with the [scientific calculator](/calculators/scientific) until the keys feel automatic.

## Mistake 3: falling for the trap answer

SAT multiple choice is designed with your mistakes in mind. The wrong answers are not random. They are the answers you get when you make the classic errors.

If a question asks for the area and you compute the perimeter, guess what one of the choices is? The perimeter. If you forget to convert minutes to hours, there is a choice waiting with exactly that wrong number. The test writers know every shortcut your brain wants to take, and they priced each one.

The fix: when your answer matches a choice *immediately*, be suspicious, not relieved. Especially check units, and especially check whether you answered for x when they asked for 2x (see mistake 1, they really love that one). Slow down on the last step. The trap answers are always in the final step, never the first.

## Mistake 4: running out of time on hard questions

Here is how most students spend their SAT math section: they hit a hard question early, wrestle it for six minutes, get it wrong anyway, and then rush the last ten questions, making careless errors on problems they actually knew how to do.

That is backwards. Every question is worth the same single point.

The fix: **two-pass strategy.** First pass: answer everything you can do confidently, circle the rest, and keep moving. Second pass: go back to the circled ones with your remaining time. The easy points you bank in pass one are worth exactly as much as the hard ones, and you will attempt the hard ones with a calmer brain. I tell my students: the test rewards the student who attempts 40 questions carefully, not the one who attempts 54 frantically.

## Mistake 5: sloppy algebra under pressure

Under time pressure, your handwriting gets worse, your steps get skipped, and sign errors multiply. The SAT does not give partial credit. A sign slip in step two means the whole question is wrong, no matter how good your method was.

The fix is boring and it works: write every step, line them up vertically, and keep the equals signs in a column. When you finish a question, plug your answer back into the original equation if you have ten seconds. That one check catches most slips. The [linear equation article](/articles/how-to-solve-a-linear-equation) has the clean step-by-step format worth copying.

## The week before: what actually helps

Do not learn new topics the week before the SAT. You will not master a new topic in five days, and the stress will cost you more than the topic is worth. Instead:

1. Take one full timed practice section and grade it.
2. Sort every mistake into one of the five categories above.
3. Drill your top two categories with targeted practice.

Most students discover that 70 percent of their lost points come from just two of these five mistakes. Fix those two, and your score jumps without learning a single new formula.
